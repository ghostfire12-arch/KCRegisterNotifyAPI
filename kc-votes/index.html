Step by Step

How to Register

Missouri makes it straightforward. Follow these four steps and you're ready to vote.

01

Check Your Eligibility

You must be a U.S. citizen, Missouri resident, and at least 17½ years old (18 by Election Day). Felony sentences must be fully completed, including parole and probation.

02

Gather Your Documents

Have your Missouri driver's license or state ID number ready. No ID? Your last 4 digits of your Social Security Number will work on the online registration form.

03

Submit Your Registration

Register online at s1.sos.mo.gov, by mail using the National Voter Registration Form, in person at the Jackson County Board of Elections, or at the DMV when updating your license.

04

Confirm Your Status

After submitting, verify your status at the Missouri Voter Registration Lookup. You'll also receive a voter registration card in the mail within 2 weeks.

Polling Hours

All Kansas City polling places are open 6 AM – 7 PM on Election Day. If you're in line by 7 PM, you have the right to vote.

What to Bring

Missouri requires photo ID. Bring your driver's license, passport, or any government-issued photo ID. First-time voters may also use a utility bill or bank statement.

Vote Absentee

Can't make it in person? Missouri voters can request an absentee ballot without needing a reason. Request yours at least 6 days before the election.

Missouri does not offer same-day voter registration. If you miss the 28-day deadline, you'll need to wait until the next election. This is exactly why we recommend signing up for deadline reminders — so you're always prepared well in advance.

In Missouri, you can register and vote once you've completed your entire sentence, including parole and probation. If you are currently incarcerated for a felony, you are not eligible. Once released and off supervision, your voting rights are automatically restored — no petition required.

Check your registration status at voteroutreach.sos.mo.gov. Enter your name and date of birth to see your current registration status, party affiliation, and assigned polling location. It only takes about 30 seconds.

As a registered KC voter, you can vote in federal elections (President, Senate, House), state elections (Governor, state legislature), and local elections (Mayor, City Council, School Board, ballot measures). Local elections often have the most direct impact on your neighborhood and daily life — and tend to be decided by very small margins.

Missouri does not have traditional early voting, but you can vote absentee in person starting 6 weeks before an election at the Jackson County Board of Elections at 2841 McGee Trafficway, Kansas City. No reason is required to request an absentee ballot in Missouri.
